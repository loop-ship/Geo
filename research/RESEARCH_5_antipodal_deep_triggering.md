# Antipodal & Deep-Earthquake Triggering: Implementation Guidance for a Pole-Shift Simulation "Effects Layer"

## TL;DR
- **Dynamic (remote) triggering is established mainstream science** and should be implemented confidently: a large earthquake's surface waves trigger seismicity globally where peak dynamic stress exceeds ~a few kPa, instantaneously to days later — but the *specific* claim that quakes preferentially trigger DEEP earthquakes near the exact antipode is **contested/fringe**, resting essentially on one 2018 statistical paper (O'Malley et al.).
- **Implement two distinct rules with honest labels:** (A) a well-supported "global dynamic triggering" rule (rate bump scaling with magnitude, decaying with distance, lasting hours–days, biased toward shallow events and Love-wave radiation lobes); and (B) an optional, clearly-flagged "antipodal-focusing bonus" — a modest extra rate bump in a ~30° cap around the antipode — labeled CONTESTED, with deep-earthquake preference labeled SPECULATIVE.
- **The pole-shift → deep-earthquake link is beyond published science.** Reorientation (membrane) stress is real and large (hundreds of MPa for big reorientations) but affects the lithosphere in a known quadrupole pattern; no literature connects bulge reorientation or rotation-rate change to deep (300–700 km) slab seismicity, and deep quakes are demonstrably the LEAST susceptible to small-stress triggering.

## Key Findings

**1. Antipodal earthquake-earthquake link (CONTESTED → FRINGE for the deep-specific claim).** Central reference: O'Malley, Mondal, Goldfinger & Behrenfeld (2018), *Scientific Reports* 8:11611 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-018-30019-2). Analyzing 1973–2016 global data: "The region with the greatest chance of induced earthquakes following all source events is on the opposite side of the earth, within 30 degrees of the antipode." M≥6.5 sources raise the rate of M≥5.0 events worldwide over a 3-day window; secondary peak ~45°, minimum ~90°; p-values commonly <0.01. They do NOT claim a deep-earthquake preference — that is an extrapolation. The lead author concedes the physics is "still largely speculative." Older Russian line (Guglielmi & Zotov 2012/2013) reports a weak "antipodal effect." Contested: small large-source sample; constant background seismicity; 3-day windows risk unrelated events. Antipodal-focusing study (Retailleau et al. 2014) states only "cannot rule out antipodal triggering."

**2. Physics of antipodal focusing (ESTABLISHED as a wave phenomenon; triggering significance UNPROVEN).** Surface waves on all great-circle paths re-converge at the antipode; body waves focus through the core. Amplification modest in reality: Retailleau et al. (2014, *GJI* 199:1030) ~15× at 20–50 s with USArray; White (2002) ~100× theoretical / ~25× observed; Meschede, Myhrvold & Tromp (2011, *GJI* 187:529, Chicxulub analog) found ellipticity/heterogeneity reduce peak antipodal displacement ~4×, but interference "channels" with stresses 5× surroundings still form (peak ~15 bar, strain ~2×10⁻⁵ for an impact). Surface waves (~3.5 km/s) reach the antipode (~20,000 km) in **~3 hours**. S-waves cannot reach the antipode directly (liquid outer core; S shadow 104°–180°), so any antipodal coupling rides surface waves and core-transiting P phases. Planetary-impact analog (Mercury's Caloris chaotic terrain; lunar Mare Ingenii antipodal to Imbrium) is the best plausibility anchor — but those involve impact energies far beyond any quake, and even there the seismic-focusing origin is now disputed.

**3. Dynamic / remote triggering (ESTABLISHED MAINSTREAM).** Hill et al. (1993, *Science* 260:1617): Landers M7.3 triggered seismicity to 1250 km. Velasco et al. (2008, *Nature Geoscience* 1:375): global ubiquity after the 2004 M9.2 Sumatra quake. van der Elst & Brodsky (2010, *JGR* 115:B07311): triggering rate scales linearly with peak dynamic strain, down to ~3×10⁻⁹ (≈0.1 kPa at 30 GPa); working thresholds a few kPa. Brodsky & van der Elst (2014, *Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci.* 42:317) is the authoritative review. Pollitz et al. (2012, *Nature* 490:250): after the 2012 event "the rate of occurrence of remote M ≥ 5.5 earthquakes (>1,500 kilometres from the epicentre) increased nearly fivefold for six days... extended in magnitude to M ≤ 7," along Love-wave radiation lobes where dynamic shear strain exceeded 10⁻⁷ for ≥100 s; great thrust events ~one-third that rate, lasting two days. Lags instantaneous to days; probability scales up with magnitude, down with distance.

**4. Why deep earthquakes are the WRONG target.** Deep-focus quakes (300–700 km) occur by transformational/anticrack faulting (metastable olivine→spinel; Green & Burnley 1989, *Nature* 341:733). They have **low aftershock productivity** (Frohlich 1987, *JGR* 92:13944 — least common 100–450 km) and are **NOT tidally triggered** (Curchin & Pennington 1987). High pressure suppresses ordinary frictional response. So a rule that preferentially triggers DEEP quakes from small remote/antipodal stresses runs against the evidence. (Minor caveat: warm/fluid-rich intermediate-depth nests like Bucaramanga show weak tidal correlation — region-specific minority result.)

**5. Pole-shift / bulge reorientation (lithospheric stress REAL; deep link UNPUBLISHED).** Melosh (1980, *Icarus* 44:745): "rotation of the lithosphere over the equatorial bulge by 25° produces membrane stresses of several kilobars" (~300–500 MPa for Mars). Global pattern is a degree-2 quadrupole: extension at the reorientation-path endpoint and its antipode, thrust at the old poles, strike-slip between; stress grows with angle. For Earth the same membrane framework applies (Creveling et al. 2012; effective elastic thickness ~10 km) giving bars to ~tens of MPa for tens-of-degrees TPW — but rapid/large TPW on Earth is contested and slow (Phanerozoic ≤3°/Myr), and the membrane signal is normally swamped by plate-tectonic stress. **No published work connects bulge reorientation to DEEP slab seismicity** — slabs *drive* TPW, not the reverse. LOD correlations (Anderson 1974; Bendick & Bilham 2017; Bilham et al. 2022 Caribbean) are statistically provocative but the stresses (~14 Pa from 1 ms LOD up to ~0.4 MPa in Sottili et al. 2014) are far below triggering thresholds; concern shallow equatorial boundaries, not deep events; no satisfactory causal mechanism. Favored idea: *synchronization* of already-critical faults by weak forces, not magnitude-based triggering.

**6. Tidal triggering — the calibration anchor (ESTABLISHED for small-stress modulation).** Cochran, Vidale & Tanaka (2004, *Science* 306:1164): Earth tides trigger shallow thrust earthquakes; rate varies by a factor of 3 with tidal stress, best correlation at μ=0.4, strongest where peak tidal Coulomb stress exceeds ~20 kPa. Métivier et al. (2009, *EPSL* 278:370): small/shallow events most susceptible. Lesson: small periodic stresses (~1–10 kPa, up to ~20 kPa) measurably modulate seismicity as a continuous function of stress (often no hard threshold), but the effect is small and concentrated in shallow, critically-stressed, favorably-oriented faults — NOT deep events.

## Implementable scaling relationships
- Dynamic-triggering rate change ∝ peak dynamic strain (linear). Peak dynamic stress ≈ shear modulus (~30 GPa) × peak strain; ~0.1 kPa floor, a few kPa practical, tens of kPa robust.
- No sharp threshold; probability is a continuous increasing function. ~50% (possible) to ~20% (probable) of regional catalogs; 86% of plate boundaries show some susceptibility.
- Magnitude/time: largest strike-slip (M8.6, 2012) → ~5× global M≥5.5 rate for 6 days; great thrust (M≥8.5) → ~1/3 of that for ~2 days. Surface-wave arrival at antipode ≈ 3 hours; delayed triggering of larger events lags >8 hours.
- Antipodal cap (O'Malley 2018): 30° cap around the antipode (secondary ~45°, minimum ~90°); near-source aftershock zone excluded as 3× rupture length (~25° for the largest events).
- Reorientation stress (Melosh quadrupole): extension at path endpoints + antipode; compression (thrust) at 90°-offset old poles; magnitude scales with flattening × angle, hundreds of MPa for large (25°) reorientations.

## Recommendations
**Stage 1 — established rule (label MAINSTREAM):** "Global dynamic triggering." When a source M ≥ ~6.5 occurs, raise triggering probability everywhere for hours–to-~3–6 days, bump ∝ peak dynamic strain (up with magnitude, down with distance). Convert with μ≈30 GPa into the same machinery as the Coulomb rule but as a transient stress. Bias toward shallow (<50 km), critically-stressed faults and (for great strike-slip) Love-wave lobes. Calibrate the floor with the tidal anchor; benchmark the bump on Pollitz et al. (2012): ~5× global M≥5.5 for the largest strike-slip; ~1/3 for great thrusts.

**Stage 2 — antipodal-focusing bonus, OPTIONAL and FLAGGED (label CONTESTED):** a modest extra bump in a 30°-radius antipodal cap (plus a smaller ~45° feature), peaking ~3 hours after the source, persisting up to 3 days (O'Malley 2018). Keep it small vs the near-field effect; toggle defaulting OFF/low-weight. **Do NOT make it deep-specific by default** — that has no published support and contradicts the deep-quake evidence. Any "deep antipodal" mode must be labeled SPECULATIVE with minimal weight.

**Stage 3 — pole-shift coupling (MAINSTREAM-framework for lithosphere; SPECULATIVE for deep):** drive the local Coulomb rule with the Melosh quadrupole reorientation field (extension at pole-path endpoints + antipode; thrust at old poles), scaling with flattening × angle. For large/rapid reorientation this reaches hundreds of MPa — apply to the lithosphere/shallow crust. Do NOT auto-trigger deep slab seismicity from reorientation/rotation-rate; if modeled, flag SPECULATIVE, keep stresses modest, document the lack of support.

**Thresholds that would change these:** (a) a rigorous, declustered replication of O'Malley confirming antipodal excess (especially deep preference) at p<0.01 → promote Stage 2; (b) a physical mechanism linking LOD/TPW stress to deep seismicity → revisit Stage 3 deep coupling; (c) a future great strike-slip event reproducing Pollitz-style global M≥5.5 triggering → raise confidence in Stage-1 scaling.

## Caveats
- The antipodal-deep claim hangs on essentially one contested study (O'Malley 2018); treat reliant output as illustrative, not predictive.
- Antipodal focusing is real for *waves*, but earthquake-sourced dynamic stresses at the antipode are almost certainly too small to systematically trigger failure, let alone deep failure; the impact analogs involve far greater energies and even their seismic-focusing origin is partly disputed.
- Deep earthquakes are the least aftershock-productive and least tidally susceptible class — the poorest candidates for small-stress remote/antipodal triggering. Targeting them specifically is the weakest link.
- LOD/rotation correlations are statistically interesting but mechanistically unexplained; implement only as a weak synchronization modulator, if at all.
- **Conflict to surface in-engine:** O'Malley (antipodal excess of large events) is in direct tension with Parsons & Velasco (2011, *Nature Geoscience* 4:312), who found an *absence* of remotely triggered LARGE earthquakes beyond the mainshock region. Show both when the antipodal rule is active.
